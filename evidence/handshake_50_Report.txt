REPORT

Setup: 50 runs per protocol, 25 ms one-way delay with netem (baseline RTT about 51 ms from ping), loss 0%, and a warm-up run that isn’t counted.

Measurement			Mean (ms)	Median (ms)	Std dev (ms)
TCP handshake			65.0		58.4		25.9
TLS 1.3 handshake (over TCP)	82.2		73.2		28.6
TCP + TLS 1.3 total (HTTP/2)	147.1		135.3		42.0
QUIC incl. TLS 1.3 (HTTP/3)	86.2		81.1		19.6
Gap (TCP+TLS minus QUIC)		5	4.2	


TCP + TLS 1.3 needs about 2 round trips (135.3 ms median, about 2.6 times the baseline RTT of 51 ms).
QUIC needs about 1 round trip (81.1 ms median, about 1.6 times the baseline RTT).
The gap of 54.2 ms is about one RTT, which matches the theory that QUIC removes one round trip.
QUIC’s handshake was about 40% faster (54.2 / 135.3).

Limitations: the VM adds timing noise (see the high standard deviations), and aioquic runs in Python, so its encryption work adds a few milliseconds above the pure network delay. That is why we quote medians.

Conclusion: “Without any packet loss, HTTP/3’s single-round-trip handshake saves about one RTT compared with HTTP/2 over TCP + TLS 1.3.”
